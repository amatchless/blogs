What do SEO packages in Delhi typically include?

Most SEO packages in Delhi bundle five kinds of work: a technical audit and fixes, on-page optimisation of key pages, local SEO (Google Business Profile, citations, reviews), new content, and monthly reporting. Higher tiers add link building, more content, e-commerce or multi-location work, and optimisation for AI answers.

In practice, the line items on a quote fall into these buckets:

  • Technical SEO: site speed, mobile issues, broken pages, indexing and crawl problems, schema markup.
  • On-page SEO: keyword research, page titles, headings, internal links and copy for your service or product pages.
  • Local SEO: Google Business Profile setup and posts, local citations, location pages and a plan for reviews (our guide on how to get more Google reviews covers that part).
  • Content: new blog posts, guides or landing pages aimed at searches your customers make.
  • Off-page: earning mentions and links from relevant sites, not buying them in bulk.
  • Reporting: a monthly report built on Google Search Console and Analytics data you can see yourself.

The tier you pay for decides how much of each bucket you actually get.

Affordable vs cheap SEO in Delhi: where the risks start

Affordable SEO services in Delhi and cheap SEO services in Delhi are not the same thing. Affordable means the price fits your stage and you still get real, visible work. Cheap means the price is so low that real work can't fit into it.

The maths is simple. A ₹3,000 package doesn't pay for many hours of a skilled person's time, so something has to give. Usually it is replaced by automated reports, copied content or bulk links. ZestRank calls packages at ₹2,000 to ₹5,000 a month a red flag, and Beyond DigitA links offers of ₹5,000 or less with low-quality tactics.

Low cost SEO in Delhi can still make sense for a small shop that needs its Google Business Profile set up and a few pages fixed. Just buy it as a small, defined job, not as "complete SEO".

Red flags: guaranteed rankings, bulk backlinks, no reporting access

  • Guaranteed rankings. Google's own guide to hiring an SEO says: "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google." If someone guarantees first place, find someone else.
  • Bulk backlinks. "500 backlinks a month" usually means links built for rankings, not for people. Google's spam policies define link spam as links created "primarily for the purpose of manipulating search rankings", including paying for links. That can lower your rankings instead of raising them.
  • No access to your own data. You should own your Search Console, Analytics and Google Business Profile, and give the agency access, not the other way round.
  • Secret methods. Google warns against SEOs who are secretive or "won't clearly explain what they intend to do."
  • Reports with no source. A PDF of rankings for keywords nobody searches is not proof of progress.
  • Long lock-ins with no exit. A 12-month contract you can't leave, with no deliverables list, protects the agency, not you.

How to compare two SEO quotes like for like

Put both quotes side by side and ask the same ten questions. If one agency can't answer them in writing, that is your answer.

  1. What exactly do I get each month? Number of pages optimised, articles written, technical fixes, and local SEO tasks.
  2. Who does the work? A named senior person, a junior team, or an outsourced partner?
  3. What did you find in my site already? A good agency looks at your site before quoting.
  4. Which keywords, and why? They should be searches your buyers make, not vanity terms.
  5. How will you get links? Ask for examples. "We have a network" is a warning sign.
  6. Do you follow Google Search Essentials? One of the questions Google itself suggests asking.
  7. What results do you expect, and in what timeframe? Also from Google's list. Expect a range, not a promise.
  8. Do I own all the accounts and content? Search Console, Analytics, Business Profile, and every article you pay for.
  9. What does the monthly report show? Clicks, enquiries and sales from search, not just rankings.
  10. How do I leave? Notice period, and what you keep if you go.

Then convert both quotes into the same unit: rupees per deliverable. A ₹20,000 plan with four articles and real outreach can be better value than a ₹12,000 plan with an automated report.
