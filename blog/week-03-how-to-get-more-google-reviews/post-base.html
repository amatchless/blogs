Most local businesses in India don't have a reviews problem. They have an asking problem.

The clinic down the road has 40 years of happy patients and 23 Google reviews. The salon next door opened last year and already has 410. The difference is rarely the quality of the work. One of them sends every customer a polite WhatsApp message with a link, and the other hopes people will remember to search for them, find the profile, tap the stars and write something, all on their own.

This guide shows you how to get more Google reviews the right way: how to find your review link and QR code, when and how to ask (with copy-paste WhatsApp and SMS templates in English, Hinglish and Hindi), how to reply to good, bad and fake reviews, what Google's policy bans, and how reviews feed into your Google Maps ranking. It ends with a 30-day plan you can start this week.

1. Why Google reviews matter more than ever in 2026

Google reviews do two jobs at once. They help you show up (on Google Maps and in the local pack on Search), and they help you get chosen once someone sees you next to three competitors.

BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 is the largest long-running study of how people use local reviews. It surveys US consumers, but the habits match what we see with Indian searchers on their phones:

  • 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, and 41% "always" read them, up from 29% a year earlier.
  • 47% won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and 31% will only use a business rated 4.5 stars or higher.
  • 74% only care about reviews written in the last three months. Old reviews stop working.
  • 78% were asked for a review in the last year, and most of those who were asked went on to leave one. Asking works.
  • 89% expect owners to respond, and 42% are unlikely to use a business that ignores its reviews.
  • Use of ChatGPT and other AI tools for local recommendations jumped from 6% to 45% in a year. AI assistants lean heavily on review text when they decide which business to suggest.

What this means for an Indian shop, clinic, salon, restaurant or coaching centre: reviews are not a one-time project. You need a small, steady system that brings in a few honest reviews every week, and someone who replies to each of them.

2. How Google reviews affect your Google Maps ranking

Google's own help page says local results are based mainly on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your Business Profile matches what someone searched for. Your category, services, description and attributes drive this.
  • Distance: how far you are from the searcher, or from the area they searched. You can't change this, which is why "near me" results differ street by street.
  • Prominence: how well known you are. Google says this includes "how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have", and that "more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking."

So reviews are one lever among several. Here are all the levers you control, in order of what we'd fix first.

Lever What to do Why it helps
Primary category Pick the most specific one ("Dental clinic", not "Clinic"). Add a few secondary categories Biggest single relevance signal
Name, address, phone (NAP) Same spelling on your profile, website, Justdial, IndiaMART, Facebook and Instagram. No keyword stuffing in the name Consistency builds trust; stuffing can get a profile suspended
Verification and hours Verify the profile, keep hours (and festival hours) up to date Google says complete, accurate profiles are more likely to show
Reviews A steady flow of genuine reviews, every one replied to Prominence, click-through and trust
Photos and posts Real photos of the place, team and work; a post every week or two Signals an active business, improves clicks
Services and products List each service with a short description More searches your profile can match
Website and links A fast site with a page per service and your city in the text; local links (associations, press, directories) Relevance and prominence

Third-party studies by local SEO tool makers also find that review count, how recent the reviews are, and the words customers use in them all go hand in hand with higher map positions. These are correlations, not rules Google has published, and you can't ask customers to use particular words (more on that in section 8). The practical lesson is simple: more genuine reviews, arriving steadily, from customers who describe what you actually did.

3. Step 1: Get your Google review link and QR code

Don't send people to "search for us on Google". Every extra tap loses reviewers. Send them straight to the review box.

On a computer (Google says review QR codes can only be made in a computer browser, not on mobile):

  1. Sign in to the Google account that manages your Business Profile and search for your business name on Google.
  2. Select Read reviews, then Get more reviews.
  3. Select Copy to copy your short review link.
  4. To get the QR code, right-click it and choose Save image as.

Check it works. Open the link in an incognito window on your phone. It should open your profile with the star rating box ready. Then save the link in three places: your WhatsApp Business quick replies (type /review to insert it), your billing or booking software, and a note on the front-desk phone.

One limitation to know: customers must be signed in to a Google account to leave a review. Almost every Android phone user in India already is, but some iPhone users and older customers may not be. Don't push them. Thank them and move on.

4. When and how to ask for Google reviews

The best time to ask is right after the customer has felt the value, while they still have their phone in their hand. Ask too early and there's nothing to say. Ask a week later and they've forgotten.

Business Best moment to ask Best channel
Salon, spa, makeup artist 1 to 3 hours after the appointment WhatsApp from the salon's number
Clinic, dentist, physio The same evening, or after the follow-up visit WhatsApp or SMS. Keep it general; never mention the treatment
Restaurant, café, sweet shop At the table or counter while they pay QR on the table tent or bill; WhatsApp for phone orders
Coaching centre, school, academy After a milestone: first month, a test result, course end WhatsApp to parents and adult students
Retail shop, showroom At billing, and on the e-bill Counter QR standee plus SMS or WhatsApp e-bill
Home services (AC repair, pest control, interiors) When the job is signed off, or 1 day later WhatsApp from the technician's or office number
D2C brand with a store or office 2 to 3 days after delivery WhatsApp or email

Five rules that make any ask work better:

  • Ask everyone, not just the smiling ones. Asking only happy customers is "review gating", and Google bans it (section 8). It also makes your rating look fake.
  • Make it personal. Use their name and mention the visit ("your haircut today", "Riya's tuition this month"). A message from a person gets far more replies than one from "Team XYZ".
  • Send the direct link. Never "search for us".
  • Ask once, remind once. One gentle reminder after 3 to 4 days is fine. After that, stop.
  • Make it easy to answer in any language. Many customers would rather write in Hindi or Hinglish. Say that's welcome.

5. WhatsApp and SMS review request templates

Copy these, change the parts in brackets, and save them as quick replies in WhatsApp Business. Each one asks for honest feedback, doesn't suggest a star rating, and doesn't tell the customer what to write, which keeps you inside Google's rules.

English (WhatsApp)

Hi [Name], thank you for visiting [Business] today. We'd love to hear how it went. If you have a minute, could you share your honest feedback on Google? It helps other people in [area] choose, and helps us improve. [review link] – [Your name], [Business]

Hinglish (WhatsApp)

Hi [Name] ji, aaj [Business] aane ke liye thank you! Aapka experience kaisa raha? Agar 1 minute ho, toh please Google par apna honest feedback likh dijiye. Isse doosre logon ko sahi choice karne mein help milti hai. [review link] 🙏 – [Your name], [Business]

Hindi (WhatsApp)

नमस्ते [Name] जी, आज [Business] आने के लिए धन्यवाद! आपका अनुभव कैसा रहा? अगर आपके पास 1 मिनट हो, तो कृपया Google पर अपनी सच्ची राय लिखें। इससे दूसरे लोगों को सही चुनाव करने में मदद मिलती है। [review link] 🙏

SMS (short, under 160 characters)

Thanks for choosing [Business], [Name]! Please share your honest feedback on Google: [review link] – [Business]

One reminder, 3 to 4 days later

Hi [Name], just a gentle reminder in case you missed it. Your feedback on Google really helps a small business like ours: [review link]. No worries if you're busy. Thank you!

What to avoid in any template: "Please give us 5 stars", "Mention Dr Sharma by name", "Write that we're the best salon in Rohini", "Show us your review and get 10% off". Each of these breaks Google's policy.

A few practical notes for India:

  • WhatsApp Business app (free) is fine for sending these by hand, one at a time, to customers who have messaged or bought from you.
  • To send automatically after every bill or booking, you need the WhatsApp Business API through a provider, with a pre-approved message template and a per-message charge from Meta. We break down those costs in our AI automation guide for Indian small businesses.
  • Business SMS in India needs DLT registration (a registered sender ID and approved templates) with your telecom operator. Most billing and SMS tools handle this for you.
  • Only message customers who gave you their number and expect to hear from you. India's data-protection law (DPDP) applies to customer numbers too.

6. In the shop: QR standees, bills and packaging

For walk-in businesses, a printed QR code catches the customers you'll never message. Google itself suggests printing the review QR code for your store and adding the link to receipts and thank-you messages.

Where it works best:

  • A counter standee at billing. A5 or A6, at eye level next to the payment QR (customers already have their phone out for UPI). Keep the line short: "Enjoyed your visit? Tell others on Google." with the QR and your Google rating.
  • Table tents in restaurants and cafés, and a small card in the waiting area of clinics and salons.
  • The printed or e-bill. Add the short review link or a small QR at the bottom of every invoice.
  • Packaging and delivery. A small card in the bag or box, for shops that deliver locally.
  • Your website and email signature. A "Review us on Google" link in the footer and on the contact page.

Make the QR scannable. Print it at least 3 cm wide, dark on a light background, with a white border, and test it from 30 cm away with two different phones. Laminate it; a curled, faded standee gets ignored.

Don't hover. Google's policy says businesses shouldn't require or pressure customers to leave ratings or write reviews while they're on the premises. A standee is fine. A staff member standing over a customer until they post a review is not, and neither is asking customers to show you the review before they leave.

7. How to reply to Google reviews (with templates)

Replies are public, so you're writing for the next hundred people who read the review as much as for the reviewer. Google's own advice: keep replies short, polite and personal, and don't use them to push offers. BrightLocal found 50% of consumers are put off by generic, templated replies, so treat the examples below as starting points and add one specific detail every time.

Aim to reply within 24 to 48 hours. 81% of consumers expect a response within a week, and about 1 in 5 expect one the same day.

Review type What your reply should do Example
5 stars, with detail Thank them by name, echo one detail, invite them back "Thank you, Priya! So glad the bridal trial helped you decide on the final look. See you on the big day. – Neha, [Business]"
5 stars, no text Short and warm, no copy-paste "Thanks so much, Rahul, really appreciate you taking a moment. See you next time!"
3 to 4 stars Thank them, acknowledge what fell short, say what you're changing "Thank you, Ankit. You're right that the wait on Saturday was too long. We've added a second chair on weekends. Hope to see you back soon."
1 to 2 stars, genuine Apologise, don't argue, take it offline with a named person "Sorry, Meera, that's not the experience we want anyone to have. I'd like to understand what happened and put it right. Please call me on [number]. – Amit, owner"
Clinic or health review Thank them without confirming they're a patient or naming any treatment "Thank you for your feedback. We're glad you felt looked after by our team."
Looks fake or is from a non-customer Stay calm and factual, report it, don't accuse "We can't find any record of this visit. Please contact us on [number] so we can look into it."
Abusive, spam or off-topic Report it first; reply only if it stays up Short, factual, polite

A few more rules:

  • Reply in the reviewer's language. If they wrote in Hindi or Hinglish, reply the same way.
  • Never share private details (bill amounts, medical information, phone numbers) in a public reply.
  • Sign off with a name. Google recommends a personal sign-off; it shows a real person is listening.
  • Don't stuff keywords ("best salon in Rohini Delhi") into replies. It reads as spam to people and adds nothing.

8. What Google's review policy bans

This is the part most of the ranking guides skip, and the part that gets profiles into trouble. Google's Maps content policy, which it tightened again in 2026, lists exactly what businesses may and may not do.

✅ Allowed ❌ Not allowed
Asking every customer for honest feedback, with a link or QR code Incentives for a review: discounts, cashback, free items, lucky draws, loyalty points
A QR standee, receipt link or WhatsApp message Offering anything to change or remove a negative review
One polite reminder Review gating: asking only happy customers, or sending unhappy ones to a private form first
Replying to every review, good and bad Asking for a star rating ("please give 5 stars") or for specific content in the review
Reporting reviews that break the policy Staff review quotas, or asking customers to name a staff member
Training staff to mention reviews naturally at the end of a visit Pressuring customers to review on the premises
Reviews from owners, staff, family or friends, or reviewing your own business
Buying reviews, posting from multiple accounts, or review "packages"
Leaving fake reviews on a competitor's profile

Google enforces this at scale. It says it blocked or removed more than 292 million policy-violating reviews in 2025. When it finds a pattern, it can remove the reviews, stop new reviews for a while, show a warning on your profile, or suspend it. Losing a profile with 300 reviews because someone bought 50 is a very expensive mistake.

Indian law points the same way. In November 2022 the Department of Consumer Affairs and the Bureau of Indian Standards launched IS 19000:2022, a standard for online consumer reviews. It covers platforms that publish reviews and businesses that collect them from their own customers, and the department said breaches can be treated as an unfair trade practice under the Consumer Protection Act, with complaints going to the National Consumer Helpline, consumer commissions or the CCPA. Paid or fake reviews are a legal risk in India, not just a Google one.

9. Fake and negative Google reviews: what you can do

A genuine negative review is not an emergency. Google itself says a mix of positive and negative feedback often feels more trustworthy. A 4.6 with a few honest complaints and calm replies converts better than a suspicious 5.0. Reply well (section 7), fix the cause, and let new reviews push it down.

Google won't remove a review just because you disagree with it. Only reviews that break its policy can be taken down. To report one:

  1. Open your Business Profile and select Read reviews.
  2. Next to the review, select Report and choose the reason (for example "Spam", "Off topic", "Conflict of interest" or "Profanity").
  3. Track it in Google's Reviews Management Tool. Decisions usually take several days.
  4. If Google says there's no violation and you disagree, you can file a one-time appeal for the review from the same tool.

Review bombing and extortion. If you suddenly get a burst of 1-star reviews from strangers, or someone threatens bad reviews unless you pay, don't pay and don't argue in public. Report the reviews, keep screenshots and messages, and use Google's extortion reporting form, which is linked from its help page on reporting reviews. For serious threats, file a cyber complaint at cybercrime.gov.in.

Never fight fake reviews with fake reviews. It's the fastest way to get your own profile flagged.

10. A 30-day plan to get more Google reviews

You don't need software to start. You need a link, a habit and someone who owns it. Here's the plan we'd give any local business.

Week 1: fix the profile. Check your primary category, hours, phone, address and services. Add 10 or more real photos. Copy your review link, make the QR code, and save the link as a WhatsApp quick reply. Reply to every existing review you haven't answered yet, oldest unanswered first.

Week 2: start asking. Choose one moment per customer (from the table in section 4) and one channel. Send the WhatsApp template to every customer from that day on. Print the counter standee and add the link to your bill. Brief your staff: mention reviews politely at the end of a visit, no pressure, no quotas, no names.

Week 3: build the habit. Ask your last 30 to 50 customers from the past month who haven't been asked yet. Send one reminder to anyone from week 2 who didn't respond. Reply to every new review within 48 hours. Count what's working: how many asks went out, how many reviews came back.

Week 4: review and automate. Compare your review count, average rating and Google Business Profile Performance report (calls, direction requests, website clicks) with day 1. Keep the channel that worked best. If you're sending more than a handful of messages a day, automate the request from your billing or booking system (below).

What "good" looks like after 30 days: every customer gets asked, every review gets a reply within two days, and new reviews arrive every week. If you're under 20 reviews, aim to cross 20 first; that's the line nearly half of consumers use.

Automate it once the habit works. A billing, booking or CRM tool can trigger the WhatsApp message automatically after each visit or delivery, with a reminder and a notification to you when a new review needs a reply. 11. Seven mistakes we see again and again

  1. "Search for us on Google." Always send the direct review link.
  2. Asking only the happy customers. It's review gating, it's banned, and it makes your profile look curated.
  3. Offering a discount for a review. Even a small one. It's the most common way Indian businesses break Google's rules without realising it.
  4. Buying a "Google review package". Google detects patterns, and the reviews (and sometimes the profile) disappear.
  5. Bursts, then silence. Fifty reviews in one week and nothing for six months looks unnatural and goes stale. One or two a day, every week, is better.
  6. Ignoring reviews, or replying to all of them with "Thanks!" Customers notice both.
  7. Keyword-stuffing the business name ("Sharma Dental Clinic Best Dentist Rohini"). It breaks Google's guidelines and can get the profile suspended.

FAQs

How do I get more Google reviews for my business in India? Copy your review link and QR code from your Google Business Profile (Read reviews → Get more reviews), then ask every customer at the right moment with a short, personal WhatsApp or SMS message and a counter QR standee. Reply to every review, and keep asking every week.

How do I get my Google review link? On a computer, search for your business on Google while signed in to the account that manages it, select Read reviews, then Get more reviews, and select Copy. You can download the review QR code from the same screen by right-clicking it.

Do Google reviews help my Google Maps ranking? Yes. Google ranks local results on relevance, distance and prominence, and says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. Your category, accurate details, photos and website matter too.

Can I offer a discount or gift in exchange for a Google review? No. Google bans incentives of any kind, including discounts, cashback, free items and lucky draws, in exchange for posting, changing or removing a review. Reviews posted because of an incentive can be removed and the profile can be restricted.

Is it allowed to ask customers for a 5-star review? You can ask customers to leave a review, but not ask for a particular rating or tell them what to write. Ask everyone for honest feedback instead.

How should I reply to a negative Google review? Reply within a day or two, thank them, apologise for what went wrong without arguing, and invite them to contact a named person to fix it. Never share private details in public.

Can I delete a bad Google review? Business owners can't delete reviews. You can report a review that breaks Google's policy (spam, fake, conflict of interest, offensive content). Google won't remove a genuine review just because it's negative.

How many Google reviews do I need? There's no fixed number, but BrightLocal found 47% of consumers won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and most only trust reviews from the last three months. Get past 20, then aim for a steady flow every week.
